# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Two overlapping groups, both Lao-speaking:

**Institutions** (primary): Schools, universities, companies, and government organizations that need to issue tamper-proof digital certificates to their members, graduates, or employees. They log in via the admin panel, manage recipients in bulk, and mint NFT certificates on their behalf. Their core job: issue a credential that can be independently verified forever without relying on a central authority.

**Individuals** (secondary): People receiving or self-minting achievement badges. They connect a wallet, check their certificates, and share proof of credentials. Many have no prior blockchain experience and need clear guidance through wallet connection and transaction confirmation.

Context: Laos-based users, primarily on desktop but mobile matters. Institutional admins work during office hours; individuals may check certificates at any time.

## Product Purpose

NFT LAO ຈຳກັດ lets Lao institutions and individuals issue digital certificates as NFTs on LABChain (a Lao-operated private blockchain, Chain ID 5222). A certificate minted here is permanent, publicly verifiable, and cannot be forged or altered after issuance.

Success looks like: an organization can go from login to issued certificate in under two minutes, and the recipient can share a link that anyone can verify on-chain without needing to trust NFT LAO itself.

## Brand Personality

**Bold · Innovative · Lao-first**

This is the first blockchain certificate platform built for and by Laos. The brand should carry pride in that: not borrowing a Western crypto aesthetic, not imitating generic SaaS tools, but expressing a distinctly Lao identity that happens to run on cutting-edge blockchain infrastructure.

Voice: direct and confident, not hype-driven. Explains what the product literally does. Uses Lao script as a visual strength, not as an afterthought alongside English.

## Anti-references

- **Generic NFT marketplaces** (OpenSea, Rarible): the standard "NFT platform" look (neon gradients, profile pics, floor-price stats) signals speculation and trading, not institutional credibility. Nothing should look like a marketplace.
- **Western SaaS tools** (Notion, Linear, Vercel): culturally neutral, designed for a global audience. NFT LAO should feel unmistakably Lao: the language, the design energy, and the visual references should signal local identity, not global minimalism.

## Design Principles

1. **Lao identity is not decoration.** Lao script is a visual asset, not a localization layer. Typography, navigation, and copy should treat it as first-class: the right optical sizing, proper line height for the script, and no fallback to Latin layout conventions that break the script's rhythm.
2. **Institutional credibility over crypto spectacle.** The platform issues credentials that universities and employers will trust. Every design decision should reinforce trust: clear data, verified status shown prominently, and nothing that reads as speculative or informal.
3. **Show the chain, don't hide it.** Blockchain's value proposition is transparency. Wallet addresses, chain IDs, transaction hashes, and verification status should be surfaced clearly, not buried. Users should feel the immutability, not just be told about it.
4. **Speed over ceremony.** Minting a certificate should take seconds of user time. No wizard sprawl, no unnecessary confirmation screens. The blockchain itself has latency; the UI should add none.
5. **Explain without condescending.** Many users are new to wallets and blockchain. The UI should guide them through concepts (what a wallet is, why a private key matters) with plain Lao-language copy, without dumbing down the institutional features for the expert admins.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Basic usability: legible across device sizes, functional without specialized assistive tech. Lao script requires careful line-height and font-size (Noto Sans Lao needs ~1.7 line-height at body sizes for readability). Color is never the sole carrier of information (status badges use text labels, not just color). Touch targets ≥ 44px on mobile.
